⚖️ SGA — Birth-weight Centile

Predicted weight of the newborn, normalised for the gestational week of birth, from a mid-trimester scan and maternal history.

Unlike the other tools on this site, this model does not return a risk probability — it returns a number: the predicted birth-weight centile for gestational age, with the underlying z-score alongside it. Every field is optional; anything left blank is completed with the median of that feature in the training data, and the result lists which fields were filled that way.

Mid-trimester Scan (~22 weeks)

Measurements taken at the second-trimester ultrasound.

Gestational age at the time of the second-trimester scan, in weeks (decimals allowed).
Estimated fetal weight measured at the scan, in grams.
Mean uterine artery pulsatility index at the scan, as a multiple of the median (1.0 = median).

Obstetric History

Previous pregnancies and their outcomes. The bottom row describes the most recent delivery. For a first pregnancy there is no neutral way to leave it blank — the model has no missing-value handling, so empty fields are filled with the median of parous women, which asserts a previous delivery at 40 weeks on the 50th centile. The result will say so, and should be read with that in mind.

Parity, and whether a previous pregnancy was complicated by preeclampsia.
Whether a previous pregnancy ended in intrauterine death.
Gestational age at which a previous pregnancy loss occurred, if any.
First pregnancy selected.

The four fields below describe a previous delivery. Left blank they are filled with the median of parous women, not treated as absent, so the model will be told this mother previously delivered a normal 40-week baby. There is no input that represents "no previous pregnancy" — read the result accordingly.

Years between the previous delivery and this pregnancy.
Gestational age in weeks at the most recent previous delivery.

Birth-weight z-score for gestational age of the previous baby (0 = median). Interchangeable with the centile beside it — filling in either one is enough.
